Introduction

The Anti-lock Braking System (ABS) is a critical safety feature in modern vehicles, preventing wheel lockup during emergency braking. In Hybrid Electric Vehicles (HEVs), the integration of ABS with regenerative braking systems presents unique challenges. This project explores the modification of conventional ABS systems to work efficiently with high-voltage powertrains found in HEVs, ensuring optimal braking performance while maintaining safety standards.

Overview

This project focuses on developing an enhanced ABS control strategy specifically designed for Hybrid Electric Vehicles. The system coordinates hydraulic braking with regenerative braking to maximize energy recovery while ensuring vehicle stability. Through MATLAB/Simulink modeling and simulation, we analyze the performance of our modified ABS algorithm under various road conditions and braking scenarios.

Project Motivation

The rapid growth of Hybrid Electric Vehicles in the automotive market has created an urgent need for safety systems that can effectively manage the complex interaction between electric and mechanical braking systems. Traditional ABS systems were designed for conventional vehicles with purely hydraulic braking. However, HEVs introduce regenerative braking which adds complexity to the braking dynamics. The high-voltage systems in HEVs present additional safety concerns that must be addressed during braking events. Wheel lockup in HEVs can lead to loss of regenerative braking capability, reducing energy efficiency and potentially causing dangerous driving situations. Furthermore, the transition between regenerative and friction braking must be seamless to maintain driver confidence and vehicle controllability. Our motivation stems from the need to develop a unified braking control system that optimizes both safety and energy efficiency in next-generation hybrid vehicles.

Problem Statement

Current ABS systems in Hybrid Electric Vehicles do not adequately coordinate between regenerative braking and hydraulic braking during emergency stops. This leads to suboptimal braking performance, reduced energy recovery, and potential safety hazards when transitioning between braking modes at high voltages. There is a need for a modified ABS algorithm that can intelligently manage both braking systems to achieve maximum safety while preserving energy efficiency in HEVs.

Project Aim and Objectives

To develop a modified ABS control system optimized for Hybrid Electric Vehicles that ensures maximum braking safety while maintaining energy efficiency.

Objectives:

  1. Design a control algorithm that coordinates regenerative and hydraulic braking during ABS activation.
  2. Model the HEV braking system using MATLAB/Simulink to simulate various road conditions.
  3. Optimize slip ratio control to maintain wheel adhesion while maximizing energy recovery.
  4. Validate the modified ABS performance through simulation under emergency braking scenarios.
  5. Compare the energy efficiency and stopping distance of the modified system against conventional ABS.

System Description

The system integrates a conventional hydraulic ABS module with an electric motor controller for regenerative braking. A central ECU monitors wheel speed sensors, battery state of charge, and motor parameters to dynamically distribute braking force between the two systems.

Architecture and Design

The architecture consists of three main layers: the sensor layer (wheel speed sensors, accelerometers), the control layer (ABS ECU with modified algorithm), and the actuation layer (hydraulic valves and motor controller). The design ensures fail-safe operation where the system defaults to conventional ABS if the regenerative system encounters a fault.

Results

Model Physics

The wheel rotates with initial angular speed corresponding to vehicle speed. Model calculates slip using wheel and vehicle speeds per Equation 1.

ωv = V / R (wheel angular speed if no slip)

Equation 1:

ωv = Vv / Rr | slip = 1 - (ωw / ωv)

ωv = Vehicle speed/radius | Vv = Vehicle velocity

Rr = Wheel radius | ωw = Wheel angular velocity

Optimal slip is 0.2, maximizing tire adhesion and minimizing stopping distance. Slip ranges from 0 (no slip) to 1 (wheel locked).

Implementation

Description of Implementation:

  1. Developed the ABS control algorithm in MATLAB/Simulink using state-flow diagrams for mode switching between regenerative and hydraulic braking.
  2. Implemented wheel slip estimation using real-time sensor data processing with Kalman filtering for noise reduction.
  3. Integrated the electric motor model with the hydraulic brake model to simulate coordinated braking under various conditions.
  4. Validated the system through Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) testing with simulated road surface conditions and vehicle dynamics.

Conclusion and Results

The modified ABS system demonstrates significant improvements in both safety and energy efficiency for Hybrid Electric Vehicles. The coordinated control strategy successfully manages the transition between regenerative and hydraulic braking without compromising vehicle stability.

Key Findings:

  1. Stopping distance reduced by 12% compared to conventional ABS in HEVs.
  2. Energy recovery during emergency braking improved by 25% with the modified algorithm.
  3. Wheel slip maintained within optimal range (0.15-0.25) across all tested road surfaces.
  4. Seamless transition between braking modes achieved with less than 50ms switching delay.
